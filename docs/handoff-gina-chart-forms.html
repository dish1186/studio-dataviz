Read firstWhat seven cities can and can't show

With Seattle in, reaction ranks with unusual air at 0.57 and with harmful air at −0.32. All six finished pairs went the unusual way. Seven cities is still a pattern, not proof. What the charts must let a juror see:

  • The two biggest reactions came with the least harmful air. Seattle (29×, 51 µg/m³) and San Jose (27×, 36 µg/m³) rank 6th and 7th of 7 for harm. Much of the result rests on them.
  • Eugene had the most harmful and the most unusual air but only the 4th-biggest reaction. Unusual air doesn't line up cleanly either.
  • The pair wins aren't independent. Seattle or San Jose is in 5 of the 6 finished pairs.
  • Fire news: about 15% of San Jose's and 16% of Eugene's worst-week talk mentions fire (nearby fires). Seattle's smoke came from distant fires; about 5% mention fire.

The two air measures move together (0.57 across these 7), so no chart will show them as fully separate.

2The sort toggleDish asked for changes, done

Same reaction bars, two orders: which one makes a staircase?

If people react to air that's unusual for their city, sorting by "how unusual" should also roughly sort the reaction. Sorting by "how harmful" shouldn't. Dish asked for the value being sorted on (PM2.5, or the ratio to normal) to sit above each bar. The thin bar always steps down, and the thick reaction bar either follows it or doesn't.

Sort by7 of 9 cities
Sorted by harmful air, the two biggest reactions (Seattle, San Jose) sit at the bottom. Switch to unusual: the big reactions move up, but it isn't a clean staircase. Eugene and Fresno dip, San Jose bumps.

RulesUnchanged, for reference
  • Interim numbers stay labeled "Early results · 7 of 9 cities". The result of record is the full 9-city run (build.py --full) with the same method.
  • "Lives with / rarely has bad air" are descriptive labels only, never evidence. No chronic/acute groupings, no political descriptors.
