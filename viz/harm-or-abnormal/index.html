When the air turns bad, what makes people talk about it?

Maybe people react to air that is harmful. Or maybe they react to air that is unusual for where they live. We looked at the worst smoke or haze week of nine U.S. cities, and at what each city's Reddit community said that week.

Final results: all 9 cities, using the method fixed before any posts were read.1
Three things to know

How harmful the air was

Fine particles in the air (PM2.5), averaged over the city's worst week. Above 35.5 counts as unhealthy by the EPA's standard. Every city here went above it.

micrograms per cubic meter · µg/m³

How unusual it was

That same week compared with the city's normal air for that month of the year. "3×" means three times dirtier than a typical week there.

ratio to normal · ×

How much people talked

The share of posts and comments in the city's subreddit that mention smoke, haze, air quality and similar words, compared with a normal week.

rise in air talk · ×
One example

Same smoke, different reaction

Bakersfield and Indianapolis each had a week that averaged 55 µg/m³, the same level of harm. But Bakersfield breathes dirty winter air often; Indianapolis rarely sees air like that in June.

The air was equally harmful in both cities. It was more unusual in Indianapolis, and Indianapolis talked about it about twice as much.

What drives the reaction?
People reacted more where the air was unusual, not where it was most harmful.

On the left, cities line up: the more unusual the week, the bigger the jump in air talk. On the right, there is no pattern: Eugene had by far the worst air but not the biggest reaction, and Seattle reacted most with some of the mildest air.2

Cities that live with bad air reacted less overall (rank correlation −0.37 with bad days a year). Fairbanks shows why the comparison has to be to the right month: its bad days are winter haze, but its event was June smoke, and its Junes are usually clean. Head-to-head pairs

Pairs that pull harm and unusualness apart

We paired cities using air data only, before reading any Reddit posts. In a matched pair, the air was about as harmful in both cities but more unusual in one. In a crossed pair, one city had worse air while the other had more unusual air. If unusualness drives the reaction, the city with the more unusual week should react more.

8 of 9

pairs went the way "unusual" predicts, and in none of the six crossed pairs did the city with worse air react more. Fairbanks vs Detroit was nearly a tie. Fresno vs Pittsburgh went the other way, but it is the weakest pair: their weeks were almost equally unusual (6.0× vs 5.3×).

Conclusions

What nine cities tell us

People react to unusual air more than to harmful air

Across nine cities, the jump in Reddit air talk followed how unusual a city's worst week was for that time of year (rank correlation 0.53), not how much fine-particle pollution was in the air (−0.13). Every check we set in advance pointed the same way.

Worse air alone never won

In all six crossed pairs, where one city had worse air and the other had more unusual air, the city with the more unusual week talked more. Overall, 8 of 9 pairs went the way "unusual" predicts; the one exception had two nearly equally unusual weeks.

Getting used to bad air dulls the reaction

Bakersfield, which lives with bad air most often, reacted least: 5× more air talk during a week as harmful as Indianapolis's, which drew 10×. Cities with more bad-air days a year reacted less overall (−0.37).

"Normal" depends on the season, not just the place

Fairbanks has many bad-air days, but in winter. Its June smoke was 17× its normal June air, and its subreddit reacted as strongly as cities that rarely see smoke. What counts is whether this air was normal for that place at that time of year.

A quiet reaction is not a safe week

If people respond to what is unusual, a city where smoke becomes routine may stop talking about it even as the harm stays the same or grows. Public attention can be a poor signal of public health risk, which matters as smoke seasons lengthen.

What this does not show

Nine cities give a pattern, not proof. Harm and unusualness rise together across these cities (0.73), Reddit users are not everyone, and some talk in San Jose and Eugene was likely about nearby fires rather than the air. More cities, other platforms, and how people felt (not just how much they talked) would test this further.

When the air turns bad, people notice the change more than the danger.

Notes
  1. All nine cities, run once with the method that was fixed before any event-week posts were read. Earlier versions of this page showed early results from 6, 7 and 8 cities; the pattern held at each step.
  2. "Rank correlation" (Spearman) compares the order of cities, from 1 to −1. With nine cities this is a pattern, not proof. The two air measures also move together across these cities (0.73), so they are only partly separated.
  3. San Jose and Eugene had large nearby fires with evacuations; about 15% of their worst-week posts mention fire, so some of their air talk may be fire news. Seattle's smoke came from distant Oregon fires (about 5% of its worst-week posts mention fire). Fairbanks's subreddit is small (188 posts and comments in its worst week), so its numbers move a lot with each post.
  4. Detroit's smoke week (July 2026) was national news, which may have added to local talk. Its archive is missing comments from Sunday 8 pm to midnight of that week (about 3% of a week's comments).
  5. Groups ("lives with" / "rarely has" bad air) are labels to describe the cities, added after the first results; the evidence is the rank correlations and the pre-set pairs.

Data: OpenAQ reference monitors (PM2.5); Arctic Shift archive of Reddit (posts and comments). Worst week = the Monday–Sunday week with the highest average PM2.5, 2019–2026, excluding July 4th and New Year's weeks. Normal = the median of the same calendar month, 2019–2025. MDE Studio, October 2026.
