At what point do people act?

For each city and each year: the heat or PM2.5 level at which a strong reaction becomes more likely than not (the threshold), and how often dangerous days brought one. Drag the year to see how behaviour changes.

2022

How the threshold is calculated

1. Strong reaction. A day (ER) or week (searches, news) counts as a strong reaction when its value is above that city's 75th percentile for that measure across all years. If the 75th percentile is zero, any value above zero counts. The bar is fixed, so years are comparable.

2. A curve per year. Within each year we fit a logistic curve: the chance of a strong reaction as heat or PM2.5 rises. Heat is in °F; PM2.5 enters as its logarithm, because reactions scale with multiples of PM2.5. The slope is lightly penalised so small years still give a stable answer.

3. Threshold = the level where that chance reaches 50%: the point at which a strong reaction becomes more likely than not. It is shown only when the curve rises, the year has at least 10 days or weeks with at least 3 reactions and 3 non-reactions, and the threshold falls within 10% of the worst level actually seen that year. Otherwise: “not reached”.

Ranges and the second measure

4. 95% range. Each year is resampled 300 times in blocks (7 days for daily data, 3 weeks for weekly) to keep runs of similar days together, and the threshold refitted each time. The range is shown when at least 70% of resamples give a threshold.

5. Dangerous days with a strong reaction. A simpler, every-year measure: of the days or weeks above the danger line, what share brought a strong reaction, compared with safe ones. Danger lines: felt heat 89.6°F (UTCI strong heat stress) and PM2.5 35.5 µg/m³ (EPA unhealthy for sensitive groups). A week counts by its worst day.

Weeks. Search and news weeks run Sunday to Saturday. A week's heat or PM2.5 is its worst day, since people react to the bad days.
