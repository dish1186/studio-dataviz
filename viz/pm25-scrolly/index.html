boiling frog

an investigation of human thresholds

Fig. I. Frogs in a pot of water warming slowly, seen from above.

When the air turns bad, do people react to air that is harmful, or to air that is unusual for where they live? We followed nine U.S. cities through their worst week of air and counted how much each city's Reddit community talked about it.

Plate IIA working definition
threshold
noun
a range of conditions where a decision is made; a tolerance.

Below it, people go on as normal. Above it, they can't, and they change what they do: they close windows, buy filters, stay inside, or talk about it.

Fig. 1The threshold is a band, not a line. Where it sits can move.

which makes us ask:how do we react to what's harmful vs. what's abnormal?

Plate IVSame air, different reaction
How harmful

Both weeks averaged about 55 µg/m³, far above the EPA's line of 35.5. Judged by harm alone, the two weeks were the same.

Sidebar · Fairbanks

Fairbanks sees about unhealthy days a year, most of them in summer wildfire seasons, and smoke comes in bursts. A typical June week is clean, about µg/m³. Measured against that normal, the 2022 fire week was × normal, and air talk rose ×. The comparison has to be with the right month.4

Plate VIIHead-to-head pairs

Pairs chosen from air data alone, before any posts were read

In a matched pair, the air was about as harmful in both cities but more unusual in one. In a crossed pair, one city had worse air while the other had more unusual air. If unusualness drives the reaction, the city with the more unusual week should react more.

Plate VIIIConclusion

In these seven cities, reaction followed how unusual the air was, not how harmful.

Cities used to bad air reacted less overall (rank correlation between reaction and unhealthy days a year). Relativity seems to set the threshold, even when the air is more dangerous.

Plate IXSo what?
who decides what is safe?

The World Health Organization's guideline for a day of PM2.5 is 15 µg/m³. The U.S. EPA's daily standard is 35. A typical December week in Bakersfield already sits above the WHO line. There, that level is simply normal.5

Fig. 6Normal week (ring) and worst week (dot) for the two cities, against the WHO and EPA daily limits.

When danger gets familiar, our action thresholds move up.

How have we normalized danger? How do we move from individual coping to collective action? How much more can we take?
