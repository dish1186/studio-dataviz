What happens when the changes to the world around us are so subtle and incremental that our own thresholds transform…

and we no longer react?

which makes us ask:

how do we react to what's harmful vs. what's abnormal?

We followed nine U.S. cities through their worst week of air, and studied their responses — through what they talked about on Reddit.

Meet the cities with some of the worst short-term air pollution in America

All nine made the American Lung Association’s annual ‘Worst 25’ list, and their worst weeks were selected for this study.

Across all nine cities, reaction followed how unusual the air was, not how harmful.

Cities used to bad air reacted less overall (rank correlation between reaction and unhealthy days a year). Relativity seems to set the threshold, even when the air is more dangerous.1

who decides what is safe?

The World Health Organization's guideline for a day of PM2.5 is 15 µg/m³. The U.S. EPA's daily standard is 35. A typical December week in Bakersfield already sits above the WHO line. There, that level is simply normal.2

Fig. 3Normal week (ring) and worst week (dot) for the two cities, against the WHO and EPA daily limits.

When danger gets familiar, our action thresholds move up.

How have we normalized danger? How do we move from individual coping to collective action? How much more can we take?

notes, sources and the numbers
  1. With this few cities the result is a pattern, not proof. The two air measures also move together across these cities (rank correlation ), so they are only partly separated. San Jose and Eugene had large nearby fires with evacuations; about 15% of their worst-week posts mention fire, so some of their air talk may be fire news. Seattle's smoke came from distant fires, and about % of its worst-week posts mention fire.
  2. WHO global air quality guidelines (2021), 24-hour PM2.5: 15 µg/m³. U.S. EPA 24-hour PM2.5 standard: 35 µg/m³. A weekly average is compared with a daily limit here only as a rough guide.
  3. American Lung Association, State of the Air 2026 (short-term particle pollution list). Bakersfield's worst week was a winter inversion, not smoke, so people may have seen or smelled less of it than a smoke week.
  4. A city's normal PM2.5 is the median weekly average for the same calendar month, 2019–2025, leaving out the worst week (a week counts toward the month its Thursday falls in and needs at least 5 days with readings). Normal air talk uses the normal weeks that pass the study's usability rules.
  5. Fairbanks's subreddit is small ( posts and comments in its worst week), so its share moves a lot with each post.
  7. Detroit's worst week is missing comments from Sunday 8 pm to midnight (about 3% of a week's comments); its posts are complete.
  8. Tone groups (alarm, adjusting, living with it) come from Claude's draft sorting of each air post and comment. Gina and Dish hand-checked 436 of them; the drafts matched their agreed label about two times in three, so read the splits as a guide. 26 of Bakersfield's 51 air comments come from one thread; without it, its split is about 60% alarm, 20% adjusting, 20% living with it.
  9. Reddit quotes are shown word for word, without usernames.
