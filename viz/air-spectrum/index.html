How this was made

Each city's week is its worst PM2.5 week in the study, pulled from the city's own subreddit (Monday to Sunday, in UTC). A comment or post counts as air talk only if its own words match the project's air word list; sitting in a smoke thread is not enough. One dot is one whole comment or post.

Every band is Claude's draft reading, not a checked code. Claude read each comment and chose one band; hollow dots are the ones Claude was unsure about. Comparisons with the past were sorted by direction: "it never used to be like this" counts as alarm, "it used to be worse" counts as normalizing. Forecasts, smoke maps and explanations of why the air is bad count as adjusting.

Eugene, Seattle and Pittsburgh each had more than 1,100 air comments, so a random 400 were read, spread across threads in proportion to their size; there, one dot stands for about three comments, and the percentages are weighted back to the full week. A second, blind reading of a random 10% of each week agreed with the first on . Pittsburgh's comments are missing the last four hours of the week.

Bakersfield's air talk is small (55 comments), and almost half sits in one thread that asked long-time residents how the air had affected them growing up. It pulls Bakersfield toward enduring; the second Bakersfield row shows the city without it. Percentages are each city's share of its own air talk, with "not about the air" left out.

An earlier version of this map counted short keyword passages instead of whole comments, and covered Eugene's milder August 2026 week. Re-reading those weeks as whole comments moved Bakersfield's alarm share up by 15 points and its enduring share down by 14; Eugene moved less than 10 points on every band.
