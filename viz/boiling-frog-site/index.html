We followed nine U.S. cities through their worst week of air, and studied their responses — through what they talked about on Reddit.

we looked at:

  2. 2. Response density
  3. 3. Sentiment of responses

So what did we find?

In 8 of 9 pairs, the city whose air was more unusual talked more.

↑ unusual: worst week vs. the city's normal
↓ talk: the jump in air talk on local Reddit

Across the nine cities, reaction typically tracked how unusual the air was, rather than how harmful it was.

Cities used to bad air reacted less overall. Relativity seems to set the threshold, even when the air is more dangerous.1

AI forensics

I. AI Usage Framework

Our prompt engineering strategy focused on four workflow pillars:

1) Focus on traceability

We documented every decision and every action taken in the construction of this visualization, including each group member and Claude, and documented this in an individual living data_log throughout the process as an automatic step within Claude’s processing. Not only did this help us communicate as team members, but it also helped maintain an easily accessible context window for Claude. We felt that this provided appropriate visibility of our work, and prevented miscommunications in handoffs between team members.

2) Replicability & raw data

Every data collection process (whether through API or manual data downloads) has a copy of its raw data saved in the Github, separate from any processing. We also constructed a data_descriptions.csv, which details the description, source, and units of every column of data used within our analysis and the entry of the data_log it was associated with.

3) Scripts-based construction

We relied heavily on Claude Artifacts for iteration and idea sharing. Our final submission was re-built in a Claude-enabled Python/CSS/JS/HTML, with all data within the scripts visibly linked to their data inputs in the repository for transparency and auditing purposes.

4) Auditing and verification

For our sentiment analysis, we each individually hand-tagged a sample of ~450 comments into the four selected categories (Alarm, Adjusting, Enduring, Normalizing), and then compared and aligned our individual groupings. We did this on a randomized sample, facilitated by Claude, for each of the 9 cities to ensure a fair representation. We made the decision to not include additional Claude-tagged comments within these word clouds to ensure that this part, what we see as most powerful, was without risk of AI hallucination. Beyond sentiment analysis, we found auditing Claude’s output to be relatively simple in our context, through constantly visually matching calculated values to the way they were represented within the visual construction.

Beyond these pillars, we always focused on segmented construction – either through a step-by-step build process (which we first approved) or through hand designing a visual instance for a singular city, finalizing with Claude, and first validating this output before expanding to others.

II. Bias mitigation

We tackled two key sources of potential bias – city selection and sentiment analysis – through a combination of procedural transparency and manual verification of AI outputs:

Procedural transparency – Experiment Design

A key component of our general bias mitigation strategy was structuring our data visualization as an experiment, complete with a detailed experiment design document. Here, we communicate our decisions regarding city selection, city grouping, and ‘worst week’ selection, helping ensure a logical process to our data collection and analysis and our focus on transparency.

Manual verification – Hand-tagging & Correction

The ~450 hand-tagged comments, described in ‘Auditing and verification’, were used to calibrate the remaining Claude-tagged comments within our sentiment analysis. This “calibration sample” was used to adjust the totals for the errors presented by Claude’s classification. This adjusted total was used only within the aggregate sentiment results (per-city radar charts and ridgeline plot).

III. Visualization Methodologies

Scroll story (Bakersfield and Indianapolis)

This visualization was first mocked up in Figma, then entered into Claude and animated.

City cards
  • Air Quality (spiral): Each dot represents one week from January 1, 2019 to 2025, beginning in 2019 in the inner circle and 2026 in the outer circle. Its value is the average of that week’s daily PM2.5 readings, which is then compared to WHO and EPA standards. Normal is the median weekly average for the same calendar month, 2019-2025, from which the “__X normal” of the worst week is calculated.
  • Reaction: Rise (__X more air talk) is calculated through taking the share of air-related reddit responses of a normal week, which is defined as the median of every week of the same calendar month between 2019-2025, and comparing that response level to the worst week.
  • Sentiment (radar chart): These take into account the total Claude-tagged comments, corrected through our calibration sample. Each grouping (Alarm, Adjusting, Enduring and Normalizing) is reflected on the chart as their percentage representation of the total air-related city comment pool. It is meant to reflect the tone mix within a city.
Tone hills (ridgeline plot)

The tone hills use the same tone shares as the per-city radar charts, drawn along a line to enable a clearer comparison between cities. Each line uses a bell curve in each section to reflect the share of each tone within the city’s response. The dot represents each city’s peak (its most typical tone).

Experimental design

Our Hypothesis:

Conditions that stray from the local norm will elicit more response than the actual health risk does.

  • Pos – Reddit rise correlates with ratio to normal PM2.5, not absolute PM2.5.
  • Neg – Reddit rise correlates with absolute PM2.5, not ratio to normal PM2.5.
  • Mixed – Reddit rise correlates with both, or with neither. If neither, Reddit may be too noisy a signal for this test.

Date ranges

  • 2019–2026: the range searched for event weeks and checked for PM2.5 coverage.
  • 2019–2025: the range used for anything that defines normal (the PM2.5 baseline, the Reddit baseline). 2026 is excluded from these because it is a partial year, but 2026 weeks can still be event weeks.

How do we pick the cities?

A city is included only if it passes all three checks:

  1. PM2.5 coverage: daily PM2.5 data for at least 75% of days, 2019–2026.
  2. Reddit activity: A city stays in consideration only if it has its own city subreddit (r/[city name]).
  3. Real harm: the city's worst week has an average PM2.5 above the EPA line, 35.5 µg/m³. This makes sure every city in the study actually faced harmful air. Without that, a city that didn't react might just have had nothing worth reacting to. (35.5 is EPA's 24-hour standard).

How do we pick the weeks?

Each city's worst week by absolute PM2.5: the Monday–Sunday week with the highest average PM2.5, 2019–2026. 2026 weeks are allowed as event weeks.

Why absolute and not most abnormal: picking by abnormality would build the answer into the selection.

The script notes if the event week contains July 4–5 or Dec 31–Jan 1, as a check for fireworks.

What is the control?

Within-city baseline: the median of Monday–Sunday weekly average PM2.5 for the same calendar month, 2019–2025, excluding the event week.

Definitions

  • City daily PM2.5: the [mean / median] of all listed stations in the city for that day.
  • Week: Monday–Sunday, in the city's local time. If a week spans two months, it belongs to the month holding most of its days.
  • Valid week: at least [5 of 7] days have PM2.5 data. The script lists any weeks that fail this rule and does not skip them until we approve.
  • Ratio to normal: event-week PM2.5 ÷ baseline.
  • Air-talk share: posts and comments matching the air keyword list ÷ all posts and comments in the subreddit that week.
  • Reddit rise (ratio): event-week air-talk share ÷ the median same-month weekly share, 2019–2025. This mirrors the PM2.5 ratio, so both sides are measured the same way.
  • Reddit rise (percentage points): event-week share − median same-month share. This keeps a near-zero normal from creating a huge, misleading ratio.
  • Reddit percentile: where the event week ranks among the ~30 same-month weeks, e.g., "higher than 28 of 30 normal weeks." This is how we tell a real rise from a normal week.
  • Event type: smoke, inversion, or other, assigned by [rule, e.g., checking news coverage for that week].

Reddit control protocol

  1. Order: PM2.5 event weeks are found first. Reddit is pulled only after, except for the check 2 screening pull.
  2. What we pull: for each city, the event week plus every week in the same calendar month, 2019–2025. These are the same years as the PM2.5 baseline, so "normal" means the same thing on both sides.
  3. Pull range: each month chunk is padded 3 days on each side (e.g., Aug 28–Oct 3 for September), so weeks that cross a month boundary are complete.
  4. Time zone: all Reddit timestamps are converted from UTC to the city's local time before assigning posts to weeks, so Reddit weeks match PM2.5 weeks.
  5. Subreddit: one main city subreddit per city, listed in this doc, chosen before looking at the event week.
  6. Data: posts and comments from Arctic Shift. Remove bot accounts, deleted or removed text, and duplicates.
  7. Air keyword list: written once, frozen, and used for every city. Before freezing, hand-check [50] random matches for false hits, such as "smoke" meaning cigarettes or weed.
  8. Control: the median weekly air-talk share for those same-month weeks, excluding the event week. These are the same rules as the PM2.5 control.
  9. Why share, not counts: Reddit grew a lot from 2019 to 2026. A share keeps a busier year from looking like more air talk.

What we measure for each city

A table with one row per city: city, group, event week dates, event type, event-week PM2.5, baseline PM2.5, ratio, air-talk share (density for the week), Reddit rise (ratio), Reddit rise (percentage points), and Reddit percentile.

Pairs

Built after the event weeks are found. Two kinds count:

  • Matched: similar absolute PM2.5, different ratio.
  • Crossed: the cities that were used to bad air had worse air but a smaller ratio. Bakersfield vs. Eugene is the template.

How we decide

  1. Across all cities, compare two rankings of Reddit rise: one against the ratio, one against absolute PM2.5. Whichever lines up better is the stronger driver. With such few cities, this is treated as a descriptive pattern, not statistical proof.
  2. The ranking uses Reddit rise ([ratio / percentage points]). The other measure is reported as a check.
  3. In each pair, does the higher-ratio city show the bigger Reddit rise?
  4. Note which hypothesis the evidence supports (Pos, Neg, or Mixed), including the cities that don't fit.

Data sources

  • PM2.5: OpenAQ, with station or sensor IDs listed per city.
  • Reddit: Arctic Shift (subreddit for each city).
  • Air-talk keywords: frozen list in data/lexicons/lexicon_air_v1.csv.
