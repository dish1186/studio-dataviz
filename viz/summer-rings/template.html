Detroit summer rings

Each ring is one summer, May 1 at the top running clockwise to September 30; the oldest summer sits at the centre. The soft glow marks Detroit’s hottest summer days: by actual temperature, by how far above the 1991–2020 normal they were, or by felt heat, and optionally only the days hot enough to be dangerous to people. The bubbles are the weeks and days when people reacted more than usual: pink for ice cream searches, teal for AC searches, indigo for heat-related ER visits. The more intense the reaction, the bigger and deeper the bubble.

Heat: daily maximum temperature, gridMET (Climate Engine), and its difference from the 1991–2020 normal for the date (±7-day window); felt heat = daily maximum UTCI (ERA5-HEAT) with its heat-stress category, to 2026-06-12; percentiles among Detroit’s May–September days 2022–2026. “Dangerous” = UTCI strong heat stress or higher (felt ≥ 32°C / 89.6°F). Reactions: heat-related illness ED visits per 100,000 ED visits, daily, CDC Tracking Network, HHS Region 5; Google Trends weekly search interest in “ice cream” and “air conditioner”, Detroit metro (search bubbles sit mid-week). Size and depth of colour = where the day or week ranks among Detroit’s May–September values, 2022–2026 (only the top 25% are shown by default; the cut-off = smallest bubble, the maximum = largest). Zone temperatures are exploratory estimates, not yet a finalised analysis step.
